1XYCORR · INIT

Start from the images

Point CrystalPilot at your images. It writes XDS.INP from the image header, and AutoPilot imports the one written at the beamline when it matches. Eiger HDF5, CBF and IMG frames open in the built-in viewer.

In this simulation, point at a spot to read its indices and resolution. Move the detector and the rings follow.

2COLSPOT

Find the spots

Run the pipeline step by step, to IDXREF, Integrate & Correct, or all at once, with a live log. The XDS.INP keywords are laid out as fields, and the optional ones have a switch to make them active or commented, so SPOT_RANGE is one field away.

3IDXREF

Find the lattice

The spots go into reciprocal space, and the lattice shows up. CrystalPilot pulls the outcome out of IDXREF.LP with the key values colour-coded: indexed fraction, spot and spindle deviations, refined beam centre and distance, and the Bravais table with the suggested lattice.

Look down c*. Only every other point is there: h + k is even, the C-centring.

4DEFPIX · XPLAN · INTEGRATE · CORRECT

Integrate, correct, check

The whole of CORRECT.LP is there to review, while the key values are pulled out and graphed, with a Matthews calculator, ISa, ice-ring analysis and the systematic absences. Screw axes are compared with the space group, and AutoPilot sets them from the absences.

In the 0kl plane the other columns have every l. The column at k = 0 has only even l: a 2₁ axis along c.

5XSCALE

Choose the resolution

The common cut-off criteria are marked directly on the statistics table and the graph, and each one goes into XSCALE.INP in one click. The Cutoff Preference sets which one the XSCALE autofill takes. AutoPilot has its own setting.

Click a criterion under the graph. These are the real shells of this data set.

6XDSCONV · Statistics

Table 1 is already written

Table 1 is built from the processing statistics, with the outer shell in parentheses. Copy it as TSV or LaTeX, or export a .csv. XDSCONV prepares the merged data for refinement and phasing: an MTZ when CCP4 is installed, or SHELX and XtalView formats.

The moving pictures are calculated in your browser from the cell, space group and detector geometry of this data set (EIGER 16M, C222₁, 1800 frames at 0.2°). The crystal orientation is made up, and the spot intensities are made up around its real I/σ per shell. The numbers in the panels and tables, and the screenshots, come from its actual processing.

XDS does the science

CrystalPilot does not replace XDS. It writes the input, runs XDS, XSCALE and XDSCONV, and reads what they report. XDS.INP stays a plain file, and the whole of every LP file is there to read.

Change a field here and the line changes. The editor in the program has the same fields and switches.

CrystalPilot is in beta. If something goes wrong, click ⚠ Report a problem at the top of the interface, or open an issue on GitHub.

Windows
10 (version 2004 or newer) and 11

        
XDS has no Windows version, so CrystalPilot runs inside WSL2. The one-click installer sets that up for you, and you never need a Linux terminal. Windows may warn you the first time: click More info, then Run anyway.

macOS
Apple silicon and Intel

        
Runs natively with the macOS build of XDS. Unpack the package and double-click mac/Install CrystalPilot.command (the first time: right-click, Open). Needs Python 3.7 or newer.

Linux
Python 3.7 or newer

        
Install it with a desktop entry and a crystalpilot command. Or take the single Python file, no installation: copy it into your XDS folder and run it.
